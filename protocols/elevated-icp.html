Emergency

Elevated ICP / Herniation

A blown pupil or acute drop in exam is a "treat first, image after" moment. Don't let the CT scanner delay emergent therapy.

Time-critical Treat before confirmatory imaging if herniating
01

Recognize

  • New unilateral fixed/dilated pupil, acute decline in GCS (especially motor exam or airway-protective ability), decorticate/decerebrate posturing.
  • Cushing's triad (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respirations) is a late finding — don't wait for all three before acting.
02

Immediate actions — do these first

  • Head of bed to 30°, head midline (impaired venous drainage from a rotated neck raises ICP further).
  • Airway: if GCS is low enough to threaten airway protection, intubate — but avoid hypoxia and hypotension during induction, both worsen secondary injury.
  • Hyperosmolar therapy — give this before the scan if exam is herniating: Mannitol 1 g/kg IV bolus or 3% hypertonic saline 250 mL bolus (or 23.4% 30 mL via central line if available).
  • Brief hyperventilation (target pCO2 ~30–32) can buy time as a bridge but isn't a sustained strategy — use it only as a bridge to definitive therapy.
Pearl Mannitol needs a working kidney and volume status you can tolerate diuresing; hypertonic saline needs a reliable line (ideally central for higher concentrations) and a sodium you can recheck soon after. Pick based on what's actually available at the bedside, not by default.
03

Then image and find the cause

  • Stat non-contrast head CT once initial stabilization is underway — don't delay mannitol/hypertonic saline to get there first if the exam is actively herniating.
  • Look for a surgical lesion (large hemorrhage, big territory infarct with edema, obstructive hydrocephalus) — this determines whether neurosurgery needs to be called now, not after the ICU accepts.
04

Next steps

  • ICU-level care, likely ICP monitoring (EVD or parenchymal bolt) if neurosurgery is involved.
  • Recheck serum sodium/osms after hyperosmolar bolus and periodically thereafter; avoid hypotonic fluids entirely during this period.
  • Treat fever and avoid hypercapnia/hypoxia — both independently worsen ICP and secondary injury.
Call your attending and neurosurgery simultaneously Any new blown pupil or acute herniation exam. Give the hyperosmolar bolus while you're making the calls — don't sequence these.
