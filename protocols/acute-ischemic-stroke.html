01

📞 Stroke phone rings

Information to obtain:

  • Last known well — do not trust the ED's reported LKW.
  • Age
  • Presenting symptoms
  • Blood thinners
  • Blood pressure
  • Point-of-care glucose
  • Look for possible LVO / cortical signs
  • Document the exact time you were called
Pearl Cautiously trust the ED's neurological exam, but never trust the reported last known well. Always verify the LKW yourself.
Pearl Gaze deviation + weakness on the same side → think seizure. Gaze deviation + weakness on opposite sides → think stroke.
Pearl Classic LVO findings: gaze deviation, aphasia, neglect, dense hemiparesis, homonymous hemianopia.

CSTAT — Cincinnati Stroke Triage Assessment Tool

  • 2 points: conjugate gaze deviation (≥1 on the NIHSS gaze item)
  • 1 point: cannot provide age or current month and cannot follow one of two commands (eye closure, hand open/close)
  • 1 point: cannot hold either arm up for 10 seconds before it falls to the bed

Posterior circulation — the 5 D's

  • Dizziness
  • Diplopia (double vision)
  • Dysarthria (slurred speech)
  • Dysphagia (trouble swallowing)
  • Dyspraxia (loss of coordination)

Localize the lesion

Territory & brainstem localization

Internal capsule, thalamus, cerebellum, medulla

Disabling vs. non-disabling — ask the patient directly whether the deficit is disabling to them. This is a conversation, not a fixed cutoff.

Pearl Don't neglect the neglect exam.
05

🖥 Imaging

CT head (without contrast) — look for:

  • Hemorrhage
  • Eye deviation
  • Evolving hypodensity

CTA head & neck

  • Identify LVO/MVO.
  • RapidAI (software tool showing emergent CTH/CTA-H&N images) can help flag occlusions — but review the CTA yourself, don't rely on it alone.
  • Do not delay CTA while waiting for creatinine in most patients, unless there is a specific contraindication to iodinated contrast.

CT perfusion — obtain per your attending's guidance on timing/candidacy.

Pearl Beware the "ghost infarct core" within the first ~3 hours from LKW — perfusion can overestimate core volume early on.
Pearl Excellent time to perform chart review while the patient is in the scanner. Don't delay imaging to complete chart review.
Pearl Basilar artery occlusion may present with coma, quadriparesis, locked-in syndrome, or abnormal respirations. Maintain a high index of suspicion even when the NIHSS is low.

If unsure — discuss directly with the radiology resident in the CT reading room.

06

🫼 If CT shows hemorrhage

  • Reverse anticoagulation when appropriate.
  • Neurosurgical consultation.
  • Blood pressure management — target SBP <140 mmHg where appropriate, per current ICH guidelines and institutional protocol.
  • Consider CTA when aneurysm or vascular malformation is suspected.
07

⚖ Reperfusion decision

TNK

Inclusion

  • Symptoms consistent with disabling ischemic stroke
  • Treatment can begin ≤4.5 hours from LKW
  • Age ≥18 years
  • Wake-up/unknown onset with DWI+/FLAIR− mismatch (if MRI available)

Absolute exclusions

  • Intracranial hemorrhage / SAH on CT
  • Extensive hypodensity (>⅓ MCA territory)
  • BP >185/110 despite treatment
  • Ischemic stroke <3 months
  • Prior intracranial hemorrhage
  • Severe head trauma <3 months
  • Active internal bleeding / suspected aortic dissection
  • Intracranial or spinal surgery <3 months
  • Infective endocarditis
  • GI bleed <21 days / GI malignancy
  • Blood glucose <50 mg/dL (reassess after correction)
  • Platelets <100K
  • INR >1.7
  • PT >15 sec / aPTT >40 sec
  • Full-dose LMWH <24 hrs
  • DOAC within 48 hrs (if normal renal function)
Important considerations Pregnancy is not an absolute contraindication. Major surgery within 14 days is generally a relative contraindication. Intracranial or spinal surgery within 3 months remains an absolute contraindication.

Blood pressure targets

  • Before TNK: <185/110 mmHg
  • After TNK: maintain <180/105 mmHg for the first 24 hours

TNK extended-window thrombolysis — inclusion criteria

Patient ≥18 years with symptom onset ≤9 hours, acute ischemic stroke with NIHSS ≥6 or a measurable disabling deficit (e.g., complete hemianopia [NIHSS 3], any aphasia [NIHSS 9], visual or sensory extinction [NIHSS 11], motor weakness limiting sustained effort against gravity [NIHSS arm/leg ≥2], large-vessel carotid or intracranial occlusion on imaging not amenable to mechanical thrombectomy, or any deficit judged potentially disabling by the patient and treating clinician), AND advanced imaging demonstrating infarct core <70 mL, critically hypoperfused-to-core volume ratio >1.2, and mismatch volume >10 mL.

Mechanical thrombectomy — reasons NOT to proceed

  • Onset >24 hours
  • ASPECTS <6
  • No LVO identified on CTA/MRA/DSA
  • NIHSS ≤6 or non-disabling symptoms
  • mRS >1
  • Core volume >70 mL in 0–6 hour window is unfavorable
  • Core volume within 6–24 hours symptom onset (assess favorability)
  • <80 yrs old with core volume >51 mL and NIHSS ≥20 — unfavorable
  • <80 yrs old with core volume >31 mL and NIHSS ≥10 — unfavorable
  • >80 yrs old with core volume >21 mL — unfavorable
  • CTP mismatch <1.8 in MCA, M2, distal, or ACA — unfavorable
  • Posterior circulation LVO >48 hours or unfavorable imaging
  • Brain imaging not favorable due to hemorrhagic transformation
  • Family/patient refusal
  • Occluded artery unable to be accessed (anatomical cause)
  • Allergy to contrast material
  • Equipment-related delay
  • Delay in stroke diagnosis
  • No endovascular specialist available
  • Vascular imaging not performed
  • Advanced age
08

💉 After the decision

If TNK administered

  • No written consent required.
  • Discuss risks and benefits with the patient/family.
  • Admit under Stroke.
  • Level of care: Critical Care / ICU.
  • Frequent neurological assessments.
  • BP goal <180/105 mmHg for 24 hours.
  • No antiplatelet therapy for 24 hours.
  • No anticoagulation for 24 hours.
  • Repeat CT at 24 hours before starting antithrombotics.
  • Stroke order set.

If mechanical thrombectomy

  • Keep bed slip under Stroke/Critical Care.
  • Notify NEA (neuroanesthesia).
  • Stroke order set.

Swallow evaluation

  • Keep NPO until bedside swallow screen is passed.
  • Speech-language pathology evaluation if screening fails.

DVT prophylaxis

  • Sequential compression devices initially.
  • Chemical prophylaxis when appropriate.

If no TNK / no thrombectomy — secondary prevention

  • Aspirin loading when appropriate for suspected ischemic stroke — not indicated for every patient, and may occasionally be deferred until MRI if the diagnosis remains uncertain.
  • High-intensity statin when indicated.
  • Telemetry.
  • Stroke order set.
