Emergency

Status Epilepticus

Time-based, three-stage algorithm. The clock starts at seizure onset, not at your arrival — ask the nurse or witness for the actual start time immediately.

Time-critical Adult, generalized convulsive SE Based on AES 2016 treatment algorithm
01

Recognize

  • Operational definition: continuous seizure ≥5 minutes, or ≥2 discrete seizures without full return to baseline mental status between them.
  • Get a stat fingerstick glucose and vitals while someone protects the airway and turns the patient lateral.
  • If seizure has stopped but the patient isn't waking up, consider nonconvulsive status — this still needs urgent EEG, not just observation.
02

0–5 min — Stabilize

  • ABCs, O2, turn lateral, protect from injury. Do not restrain limbs or force anything into the mouth.
  • Fingerstick glucose — if low, give dextrose (thiamine first if any concern for malnutrition/alcohol use).
  • IV access x2 if possible; send labs (glucose, electrolytes, Mg, Ca, CBC, AED levels if on one, tox screen if indicated).
03

5–20 min — First-line: benzodiazepine

Pick one based on access:

  • Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV (typically 4 mg), may repeat once
  • Midazolam 10 mg IM if no IV access — don't delay treatment waiting for a line
  • Diazepam 0.15–0.2 mg/kg IV (max 10 mg/dose) as an alternative
Pearl Underdosing benzos is the most common preventable error here. Give a full weight-based dose — a timid dose that doesn't break the seizure just adds respiratory depression risk without benefit.
04

20–40 min — Second-line: IV AED load

Seizure persisting after adequate benzo dosing — load one of:

  • Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV (max 4500 mg)
  • Fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg IV (watch for hypotension/arrhythmia, needs cardiac monitoring)
  • Valproate 40 mg/kg IV (avoid in known/suspected pregnancy or hepatic disease)

These have roughly comparable efficacy per ESETT trial data — choice is driven by comorbidities, not superiority.

05

>40 min — Refractory SE

  • This is refractory status — anesthetic-dose infusion (midazolam, propofol, or pentobarbital drip) with continuous EEG, almost always requires ICU-level airway management.
  • Get continuous EEG going before or immediately after intubation if convulsions have stopped but you're not confident the patient is back to baseline.
Call your attending now if you haven't already Any seizure still going after the first-line benzo dose. Don't wait until you're at the anesthetic-infusion stage to escalate the phone call.
06

Don't forget

  • Look for and treat the underlying cause in parallel — hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, withdrawal, CNS infection, structural lesion, eclampsia.
  • Non-contrast head CT once stabilized if no clear precipitant, or if there's focal exam findings, trauma, or anticoagulation.
  • Document a clear timeline — what was given, when, and the exact clinical response. This matters for handoff and for figuring out next-line therapy.
