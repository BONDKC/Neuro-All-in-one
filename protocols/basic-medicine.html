01 🦄 Hemodynamics▸

Hypertension

Overview
  • SBP 110–160 mmHg is generally acceptable.
  • Stroke patients may require permissive hypertension depending on the clinical situation.
  • Persistent hypertension should be treated according to patient-specific goals.
Evaluation
  • Review BP trend.
  • Confirm target BP.
  • Assess symptoms.
  • Review current antihypertensive medications.
Management

PRN IV medications

  • Labetalol 10 mg IV every 2 hours PRN — may cause bradycardia, avoid if HR is low
  • Hydralazine 10 mg IV every 2 hours PRN — does not cause bradycardia, may alternate hourly with labetalol
  • Nicardipine (Cardene) infusion — ICU only, nurse titrated

Scheduled medications

  • Lisinopril: 5 / 10 / 20 / 40 mg daily
  • Losartan: 12.5 / 25 / 50 / 100 mg daily
  • Carvedilol: 6.125 / 12.5 / 25 mg BID
  • Amlodipine: 5 or 10 mg daily
  • Nifedipine: 30–90 mg daily (or BID)
  • Hydralazine: 10 mg TID
Pearl Stroke BP goals differ from general medicine. Verify the BP goal before treating.

Hypotension

Overview
  • SBP <100 mmHg generally requires evaluation.
  • Worsening neurological deficits with hypotension should be treated promptly.
Evaluation
  • Review BP trend.
  • Assess neurologic worsening.
  • Evaluate volume status.
  • Consider bleeding, sepsis, medications, cardiac causes.
Management
  • IV fluid bolus: 1 L over ~2 hours.
  • Maintenance fluids: 75–125 mL/hr.
  • Lay symptomatic stroke patients flat.
  • Avoid excess fluids in CHF, renal failure, cirrhosis.
  • Vasopressors if needed.
  • Levophed per ICU workflow.
Escalation
  • Neuroanesthesia / ICU if pressors are required.
Pearl Mild hypotension may worsen cerebral ischemia.

Bradycardia

Evaluation
  • Check BP.
  • Review medications — recent beta blockers?
  • ECG.
  • Sinus bradycardia vs. conduction block.
  • Symptomatic?
Management
  • Atropine.
  • Pacer pads if severe.
Escalation
  • Cardiology consultation.
Pearl Treat the patient, not the number.

Tachycardia / Atrial Fibrillation

Evaluation
  • Assess symptoms.
  • ECG.
Management
  • Metoprolol 5 mg IV push.
  • Additional rate/rhythm control if needed.
Escalation
  • Cardiology.
02 🫕 Respiratory▸

Shortness of Breath

Include
  • O2 saturation
  • ABG/VBG
  • Chest X-ray
Differential
  • Pneumonia
  • PE
  • COPD/asthma
  • ARDS
  • Pleural effusion
  • Pneumothorax
  • Pulmonary edema
  • Cardiac tamponade
  • Hypoventilation
  • Anxiety

Pneumothorax

  • Chest X-ray
  • Trauma/Pulmonary consultation
  • If unstable → Rapid Response / Trauma / Neuroanesthesia

04 🍼 Nutrition▸

Tube Feeds

Include
  • Jevity 1.5
  • Start 10 mL/hr
  • Advance 10 mL/hr every 6 hours
  • Goal 65 mL/hr
  • Renal formula discussion

DHT Placement

Confirming DHT tip position on X-ray

Correct placement: tip should sit past the pylorus, within the duodenum — confirm on X-ray before starting feeds.

05 🧠 Common Neurology Complaints▸

Headache

Include
  • Rule out hemorrhage/edema
  • CT when indicated
  • Tylenol
  • Avoid NSAIDs in stroke patients
  • IV magnesium + compazine + diphenhydramine
  • IV valproate option
  • Caffeine
  • Shafer special

General Pain

Include
  • Home medications
  • Tylenol
  • Topical therapies
  • Oral medications
  • IV medications
  • Avoid NSAIDs/Ketorolac in stroke patients

Agitation

Non-pharmacologic
  • Delirium precautions
  • Reduce neuro checks
  • Sitter
  • Soft restraints
Medication options
  • Hydroxyzine
  • Ativan
  • Diazepam
  • Seroquel
  • Midazolam
  • Olanzapine
  • Haloperidol
  • Precedex infusion
Warnings Older adults. Stroke patients needing frequent exams. Labile BP.
