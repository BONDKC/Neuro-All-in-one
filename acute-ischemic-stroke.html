Time-critical Confirm last known well first

📞 Stroke phone rings

  • Get last known well (LKW) time immediately — ask directly, don't assume "found down" means unwitnessed.
  • Ask where the patient is now and their current NIHSS-relevant status (talking, moving all four limbs?) so you know what you're walking into.
  • Confirm which hospital/ED bed.
Time called 

🏃 On the way to the patient

  • Mentally confirm the LKW-to-now window — are you inside the 4.5h IV lytic window, the 24h thrombectomy window, or outside both?
  • Think through exclusions you'll need to ask about on arrival: anticoagulation use, recent surgery/bleed, seizure at onset.
  • Does this sound like a Possible Code LVO? If so, activate it — call the Access Center: 502-562-8008 (same number for both hospitals).

🏥 At the bedside

  • ABCs, fingerstick glucose first — hypoglycemia is a stroke mimic and needs correcting before you commit further down the stroke pathway.
  • Weight (for lytic dosing), two IVs.
  • Confirm LKW time again directly with patient/family/witnesses if possible — the phone report isn't always precise.

General rule: crossed/alternating signs (cranial nerve deficit on one side, long-tract deficit on the other) point to a brainstem lesion. Cortical signs — aphasia, neglect, gaze preference, cortical visual loss — point to a supratentorial lesion.

  • MCA: face and arm weakness/sensory loss > leg. Dominant hemisphere → aphasia. Non-dominant → neglect. Gaze preference toward the lesion. Contralateral homonymous hemianopia.
  • ACA: leg weakness/sensory loss > face/arm. Abulia, urinary incontinence, grasp reflex, gait apraxia.
  • PCA: contralateral homonymous hemianopia, often with macular sparing. Visual agnosia. Dominant-side occipital + splenial involvement can cause alexia without agraphia (can write, can't read).
  • Lacunar/subcortical: pure motor hemiparesis, pure sensory stroke, ataxic hemiparesis, or dysarthria-clumsy hand — notably without aphasia, neglect, or cortical visual signs.
  • Midbrain (CN III): ipsilateral CN III palsy with contralateral hemiparesis (Weber) or contralateral ataxia/tremor (Benedikt/Claude).
  • Pons (CN VI/VII, gaze): ipsilateral facial palsy ± CN VI palsy with contralateral hemiparesis (Millard-Gubler), or ipsilateral horizontal gaze palsy with facial palsy and contralateral hemiparesis (Foville). Locked-in syndrome: awake, quadriplegic, anarthric, with preserved vertical eye movements/blinking.
  • Lateral medulla / PICA (Wallenberg): dysphagia, hoarseness, vertigo/nystagmus, ipsilateral ataxia, ipsilateral Horner syndrome, ipsilateral facial pain/temp loss, contralateral body pain/temp loss.
  • Medial medulla: ipsilateral tongue deviation (CN XII), contralateral hemiparesis, contralateral loss of vibration/proprioception.
Pearl Not every brainstem stroke shows a textbook crossed pattern — localize fast off the pattern that fits best, then confirm with urgent neuroimaging rather than waiting for a perfect match.

Cincinnati Stroke Triage Assessment Tool (C-STAT) — used to help identify large vessel occlusion:

  • 2 points: conjugate gaze deviation (≥1 on the relevant NIHSS item)
  • 1 point: cannot provide their age or current month and can't follow one of 2 commands (eye closure, opening/closing hand)
  • 1 point: cannot hold either arm up for 10 seconds before it falls to the bed

For NIHSS 0–5 at presentation: if the observed deficits persisted, would the patient still be able to do basic activities of daily living and/or return to work?

ADLs to consider (BATHE): Bathing/dressing, Ambulating, Toileting, Hygiene, Eating — also assess independent swallow. This is a judgment call made with the patient and family, not a rigid cutoff.

  • Typically clearly disabling: complete hemianopia (≥2 on NIHSS vision item); severe aphasia (≥2 on best language item); severe hemi-attention/extinction affecting >1 modality (≥2 on extinction/inattention item); any weakness that can't sustain effort against gravity (≥2 on a motor item).
  • May NOT be clearly disabling in a given patient: isolated mild aphasia (still able to communicate meaningfully); isolated facial droop; mild cortical hand weakness (especially non-dominant, NIHSS 0); mild hemimotor or hemisensory loss; mild hemiataxia (can still ambulate).

Reperfusion Decision
  • Symptoms consistent with disabling ischemic stroke
  • Treatment can begin ≤4.5 hours from last known normal
  • Age ≥ 18 years
  • Wake-up/unknown onset with DWI+/FLAIR− mismatch, if MRI is available
  • Intracranial hemorrhage or SAH on CT
  • Extensive hypodensity >1/3 MCA territory
  • BP >185/110 despite treatment
  • Ischemic stroke <3 months
  • Prior intracranial hemorrhage
  • Severe head trauma <3 months
  • Active internal bleeding or suspected aortic dissection
  • Intracranial or spinal surgery <3 months
  • Infective endocarditis
  • GI bleed <21 days, or GI malignancy
  • Blood glucose <50 mg/dL (reassess after correction)
  • Platelets <100,000
  • INR >1.7
  • PT >15 sec / aPTT >40 sec
  • Full-dose LMWH <24 hours
  • DOAC within 48 hours (if normal renal function)
  • Large vessel occlusion (ICA, M1, proximal M2, basilar) on CTA
  • Disabling deficit
  • LKW ≤ 24 hours with favorable perfusion imaging beyond 6 hours
  • Reasonable pre-stroke functional status
