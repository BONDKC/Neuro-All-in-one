Urgent

Status Migrainosus

A debilitating migraine attack lasting >72 hours despite typical home therapy. The first job on the phone is ruling out a secondary headache before you treat it like "just a migraine."

Adult Rule out secondary cause first
01

Screen for red flags before treating

  • Thunderclap onset ("worst headache of life"), fever/neck stiffness, new focal deficit, papilledema, headache with exertion or positional change, immunosuppression, pregnancy/postpartum, or a history of malignancy — any of these changes the workup.
  • If present: this needs imaging (CT ± CTA, or MRI/MRV) and possibly LP before symptomatic treatment, not after.
  • If the exam and history are reassuring and this fits the patient's known migraine pattern, proceed to treatment.
02

First-line ED/floor cocktail

  • IV fluids (many patients are volume-down from poor intake/vomiting)
  • Metoclopramide 10 mg IV or prochlorperazine 10 mg IV — give with diphenhydramine 25–50 mg IV to reduce akathisia/dystonia risk
  • Ketorolac 30 mg IV if no contraindication (renal disease, GI bleed risk, recent surgery)
Pearl Dopamine antagonists (metoclopramide/prochlorperazine) are doing most of the work here, not the NSAID — they treat the migraine mechanism directly and also handle nausea. Reach for them first.
03

Refractory to first-line

  • Valproate 500–1000 mg IV or magnesium sulfate 1–2 g IV (especially useful if migraine with aura, or possible low-Mg state)
  • Consider a greater occipital nerve block if available and exam is consistent with occipital-predominant pain.
  • Avoid opioids as first or second line — they're associated with worse outcomes and rebound risk in migraine specifically.
04

Consider a short steroid taper

For truly refractory status migrainosus, a short course (e.g. dexamethasone 10 mg IV once, or a several-day oral prednisone taper) can shorten the attack and reduce early recurrence — most useful as a bridge, not a first move.

05

Disposition

  • Most patients improve enough for discharge after IV therapy — arrange a bridge plan (short taper, follow-up with headache clinic) rather than sending them out with nothing.
  • Ask about medication-overuse headache — frequent triptan/analgesic use can itself be driving a refractory pattern.
Call your attending if Any red flag on screening, pain refractory to two rounds of first-line therapy, or new/changing neuro exam findings at any point.
